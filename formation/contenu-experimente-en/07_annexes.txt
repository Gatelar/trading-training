P: The Intermediate track's seven questions are kept word for word. An eighth is added in last position: **does a scheduled announcement or a weekly open fall within this trade's expected duration?** It follows from chapter 1.3 and is answered by consulting a calendar, not a chart.

P: The seventh question — cumulative exposure — does not change wording, but changes source. Its answer now comes from the exposure table of chapter 4.4, no longer from memory. At three positions memory was enough; at five it systematically produces the lowest figure.

H3: The journal: from eight to nine fields

P: The ninth field is the **execution gap in R**, signed, recorded separately on entry and on exit. It is compulsory from the first real trade: without it the net expectancy of chapter 1.4 cannot be calculated, and the abandonment criterion goes on applying to a quantity that exists only in simulation.

P: A tenth field is offered and stays **optional**: the delay between closing a losing trade and the next order. It serves one purpose, quantifying how far the fourth rule is being got round, and it serves only those who get round it.

H3: The system document: two criteria instead of one

UL:
- The **abandonment criterion** is rewritten on **net** expectancy. That rewriting increments the version and resets the trade counter to zero, like any modification.
- A **system stopping criterion** is added: quantity, threshold, window, action, plus what becomes of open positions.
- The stopping criterion's window is **disjoint** — one evaluation per batch of 100 new trades, never rolling.
- The **starting stage** goes in if there is one: fraction, number of trades, quantity measured, end of the stage.
- The **dated decision** of chapter 6.5 goes in too, and is re-read at every monthly review.

H3: Exposure, now stated identically in both documents

P: Chapter 5.2 of the Intermediate track said that two strongly correlated 1% positions "count as 1%", which its own chapter 5.1 contradicted — three positions that lose together expose about 3%. The wording of 5.1 has been adopted, and the Intermediate track corrected accordingly.

P: So the rule is single across all three tracks: **within a group that loses together, risks add up**, and the 2% limit applies to that sum. If you hold a version of the Intermediate track from before this correction, its chapter 5.2, its chapter 5.4 and its session card are what you need to re-read.

- What is my cumulative exposure, this trade included? Answer read off the table.

Date and time · Asset and timeframe · Scenario written before entry · Invalidation and target · Size and risk in R · Highest point reached in R · Result in R · **Compliant: yes / no** · **Execution gap in R, entry and exit**
Optional field: delay between a loss and the next order.

CARD: The formulas
**Position size** = (Capital × Risk in %) ÷ Distance to the stop
**Minimum hit rate** = 1 ÷ (1 + Reward-to-risk ratio)
**Expectancy** = (hit rate × average gain) − (miss rate × average loss)
**Net expectancy** = Gross expectancy − Measured execution cost
**Standard error** = standard deviation ÷ square root of the number of trades
**Trades for a wanted precision** = (standard deviation ÷ precision)²

CARD: Exposure, beyond three positions
**1.** Group the positions by shared losing scenario.
**2.** Within a group, risks **add up**.
**3.** Between groups: above +0.3 the smaller counts half, below that they add up, opposite directions subtract.
**4.** Keep the table — asset, direction, risk, group, total — outside the platform, updated before the order.
Default refusal when the limit is reached: **the last to arrive**. Never by cutting an open position.

CARD: The starting stage
Fraction of the risk · Number of trades · Quantity measured · What ends the stage.
The stage's worst case must stay **below** your drawdown threshold. Twenty-five trades are enough to measure execution; one thousand six hundred would be needed for expectancy.
The end of the stage authorises nothing: it dates a recalculation.

CARD: The test protocol, in seven steps
Hypothesis — scope, quantity, threshold · Sample and chronological cut · Attempts allowed · Abandonment criterion · Build · **Control, looked at once** · Written and dated decision.
A skipped step voids the test. The number of attempts is noted before, not after.

CARD: The system document, and its two criteria
Scope · Entry · Invalidation and stop · Management · Exposure · Abandonment criterion, on **net** expectancy · **Stopping criterion: quantity, threshold, disjoint window, action, fate of open positions.**
Header: version, modification date, trades since. Every modification resets the counter to zero.

SEC: Glossary

NOTE: Only terms introduced by this track appear below. For the Beginner notions — R, spread, drawdown, structure, expectancy, compliance, checklist, journal — and the Intermediate ones — ATR, correlation, standard error, cumulative exposure, filter, overfitting, system — see the glossaries of those two documents.

GLOSS:
Automatic close-out :: Compulsory closing of positions when funds fall to 50% of the minimum required margin. On €1,000 at maximum leverage, it comes after a loss of 91.7%.
Decay :: Lasting disappearance of a system's edge. Indistinguishable from a bad run on any sample you will ever produce.
Disjoint window :: Evaluation window sharing no trade with the previous one. Reduces the false-alarm rate without touching the threshold.
Execution gap :: Difference between the price asked and the price obtained. Zero in simulation by construction. Counted as a fraction of the stop, never as a percentage of the price.
Exposure group :: Set of positions that lose in the same scenario. Within a group, risks add up.
Exposure table :: Sheet kept outside the platform — asset, direction, risk, group, total — updated before every order in about twenty seconds.
False-alarm rate :: Probability that a criterion fires while the system is healthy. Depends on the threshold, and more still on the window.
Market order :: Order executed at the best price available the instant it arrives. A stop-loss is a market order with a trigger.
Negative balance protection :: Guarantee, account by account, that the loss cannot exceed the funds deposited. Introduced after the debit balances of January 2015.
Net expectancy :: Gross expectancy less the measured execution cost. The only quantity to compare with the criterion once money is committed.
Out-of-sample decline :: Loss of performance observed when a rule is applied to data that did not serve to build it. Documented order of magnitude: −26%.
Price gap :: Range of prices in which no transaction took place. The stop is triggered there and then executed further away: the loss stops being bounded by your calculation.
Record :: Exhaustive, factual and exportable statement of all operations, kept for a third party. Does not replace the journal and does not follow from it.
Slippage :: See *execution gap*.
Standardised warning :: Statement by which a provider publishes the percentage of its retail client accounts that lose money. Produced by the provider itself, and checkable before opening anything.
Starting stage :: Initial reduced-risk period whose sole object is to measure the execution cost. Its end dates a recalculation and authorises nothing.
Stopping protocol :: Ninth rule: threshold, duration, resumption condition checkable from outside. A threshold less than one standard deviation from the dispersion fires on noise.
System stopping criterion :: Threshold written in advance beyond which a system in production is stopped. Distinct from the abandonment criterion, which applies to a hypothesis under test and costs only an idea.

SEC: Disclosures and sources

H3: The nature of this document

P: Educational content intended for learning. It is not investment advice, not a personal recommendation, not financial analysis in the regulatory sense, and not an inducement to open an account with any provider.

P: Leveraged trading is a high-risk activity. Most retail traders who take it up lose money: 89% of the 14,799 French clients tracked by the AMF over four years. Past performance is no guide to future performance, and the capital committed can be lost in full.

P: This track at no point recommends committing real money, presents that move neither as a culmination nor as an expected step, and recommends no amount of capital. No provider is named. No tax figure appears: chapter 6.4 is dated September 2026 and refers you explicitly to a professional.

P: The worked examples are teaching cases built to explain a point. The execution gaps in chapters 1.2 and 1.4, the records in chapter 2.4 and the sessions in module 3 are teaching illustrations, not measurements taken at a provider. The exercises take place in the TapeSense simulator.

H3: On the absence of a slippage figure

P: This document cites no measured execution gap value, because no published, checkable, independent measurement could be identified for a retail trader on this type of product. The values in circulation come from firms selling their own execution. So the track teaches the measurement, not the figure.
